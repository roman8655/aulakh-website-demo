ADHD Assessments

You deserve clear answers about how your mind works.

If you've been wondering whether ADHD explains some of what you're experiencing, you're not alone — and you don't have to figure it out on your own. We offer comprehensive, in-person ADHD assessments for teens and adults in the Halifax area, providing a thorough, compassionate path toward clarity, understanding, and next steps.

"Understanding how your mind works is the first step toward working with it, not against it."
— Our Approach to ADHD Assessments

Do You Think You May Have ADHD?

You're not imagining it — and you're not alone

Maybe you've always felt like you're working twice as hard just to keep up. Maybe deadlines sneak up on you, conversations drift away mid-sentence, or your space — physical or mental — always seems one step from chaos.

You might relate to
  • Trouble focusing, or focusing so intensely that time slips away.
  • Feeling restless, easily distracted, or constantly "on the go."
  • Difficulty organizing tasks, managing time, or following through.
  • Forgetfulness that affects your work, relationships, or daily life.
  • A sense that you're trying harder than everyone else just to keep pace.

These experiences are real, and they deserve real answers — not guesswork.

What the Assessment Involves

A comprehensive, whole-person approach — not just a checklist of symptoms
We Take a Comprehensive Approach

ADHD rarely exists in isolation, and attention difficulties can stem from many different sources. That's why our assessments look at the whole picture, not just a list of symptoms. In addition to exploring ADHD symptoms directly, we take time to understand:

  • Anxiety and obsessive-compulsive symptoms
  • Mood and emotional wellbeing
  • Physical health
  • Substance use
  • Family dynamics and upbringing
  • Significant or challenging life experiences
Why It Matters

Inattention, restlessness, and forgetfulness can have many different explanations — anxiety, low mood, stress, or life circumstances can all look similar to ADHD on the surface.

By exploring the full picture, we're able to offer a diagnosis you can trust, along with recommendations that are tailored to you, not just a label.

Please Note

Our ADHD assessments are not able to accurately differentiate ADHD from a specific learning disorder (such as difficulties with math, reading, writing, or phonetics). If you suspect a learning disorder may be part of the picture, a psychoeducational assessment is the appropriate first step, as it allows us to tell these conditions apart and recommend the right path forward. If you're not sure which type of assessment is the right fit, feel free to reach out — we're happy to help you figure that out.

The Assessment Process

A structured, thorough path toward clarity
Step 1

Reach Out

Contact us to get started — no physician referral is required. We'll answer your questions and help you understand what to expect before booking your assessment.

Step 2 · 3 Hours

In-Depth Assessment Session

You'll meet with a clinician in person for a comprehensive assessment session. This includes a thorough diagnostic interview, standardized rating scales, and a review of relevant documentation such as report cards, past learning assessments, or accommodation records. At the clinician's discretion, this may also include a Continuous Processing Test (CPT) — an objective, computer-based measure of attention.

Step 3 · 3 Hours

Scoring & Report Writing

Your clinician carefully scores all measures, integrates the information gathered, and prepares a detailed diagnostic summary. When helpful, we may also reach out to a spouse, parent, or other close contact to gather collateral information — this isn't required, but it can add valuable context to your results.

Step 4 · 1 Hour

Reviewing Your Results

You'll return for a feedback session where we walk through your results together, answer your questions, and discuss recommendations. You'll leave with a written diagnostic summary letter to keep or share as needed.

Some assessments require additional time — for example, if consultation with a caregiver, sibling, or partner is needed, or if the complexity of your symptoms calls for further exploration.
