Aulakh Psychological & Counselling Services Inc.

1. Privacy Policy

Aulakh Psychological & Counselling Services Inc. ("Aulakh Psychology," "we," "us," or "our") is committed to protecting the privacy, confidentiality, security, and dignity of the individuals who receive psychological, counselling, assessment, consultation, and related services from our practice.


This Privacy Policy explains how we collect, use, disclose, store, protect, retain, and dispose of personal information and personal health information. It also describes your privacy rights and how you may raise questions or concerns about the handling of your information.


This Policy is intended to be consistent with applicable Canadian and Nova Scotia privacy legislation, professional requirements, and ethical standards, including the Nova Scotia Personal Health Information Act (PHIA) and its regulations, applicable provisions of the Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA), and applicable standards and guidelines of the Nova Scotia Regulator of Psychology (NSRP).


Where legislation or professional requirements impose a higher standard of protection than this Policy, the applicable legal or professional requirement will prevail.

2. Our Commitment to Privacy

We recognize that information shared during psychological and counselling services is highly personal and may include sensitive information concerning mental health, physical health, relationships, family circumstances, education, employment, finances, trauma, sexuality, identity, substance use, and other aspects of an individual's life.


We are committed to:


  • Collecting only information that is reasonably necessary for appropriate care, administration, billing, legal, regulatory, and professional purposes.
  • Explaining, where required, why information is collected and how it may be used or disclosed.
  • Obtaining consent for the collection, use, and disclosure of information when consent is required.
  • Limiting access to personal information to individuals who require access for legitimate professional, administrative, legal, or operational purposes.
  • Protecting information against unauthorized access, collection, use, disclosure, copying, modification, loss, theft, or destruction.
  • Maintaining accurate and appropriate clinical records.
  • Providing individuals with access to their personal health information where required by law.
  • Responding appropriately to privacy concerns and security incidents.
  • Retaining and securely disposing of information in accordance with applicable legal and professional requirements.

Nova Scotia's PHIA specifically governs the collection, use, disclosure, retention, disposal, and destruction of personal health information and requires appropriate safeguards for health information, including electronic information.

3. Applicable Privacy Legislation

For personal health information held by the practice in Nova Scotia, the primary privacy legislation is the Nova Scotia Personal Health Information Act (PHIA) and its regulations.


PHIA establishes rules governing personal health information and provides individuals with rights concerning access to and correction of their information. It also establishes requirements concerning safeguards and privacy breaches.


PHIA has been recognized by the Government of Canada as substantially similar to Part 1 of PIPEDA. Accordingly, a Nova Scotia health information custodian subject to PHIA is generally exempt from Part 1 of PIPEDA with respect to the collection, use, and disclosure of personal health information occurring in Nova Scotia.


However, PIPEDA may apply to certain activities involving personal information, particularly where information is transferred across provincial or national borders or where the practice engages in activities to which PIPEDA otherwise applies.


The practice will comply with all applicable privacy legislation and will apply the requirements of the legislation that governs the particular information or activity.

5. Purpose for Collecting Information

We may collect, use, and disclose personal information and personal health information for purposes including:


  • Providing psychological, counselling, assessment, consultation, and related services.
  • Assessing your needs and developing treatment or service plans.
  • Ocumenting services provided.
  • Communicating with you regarding your care.
  • Coordinating care with other health-care professionals.
  • Arranging referrals.
  • Preparing psychological or other clinical reports.
  • Administering psychological assessments.
  • Scheduling appointments.
  • Processing payments and billing.
  • Communicating with insurers and benefit providers where authorized.
  • Responding to legal or regulatory requirements.
  • Maintaining professional records.
  • Quality assurance and practice management.
  • Managing complaints and privacy concerns.
  • Preventing, detecting, or responding to security incidents.
  • Meeting professional, regulatory, accounting, insurance, and legal obligations.
  • Other purposes permitted or required by law.

We will not use personal health information for a purpose unrelated to the provision or administration of care unless permitted or required by law or authorized by appropriate consent.

6. Consent

Where required by law, we will obtain consent before collecting, using, or disclosing personal health information.


Consent may be express or, where legally permitted, knowledgeable implied consent.


Where appropriate, consent may be documented through:


  • Written consent.
  • Electronic consent.
  • Verbal consent documented in the clinical record.
  • Another legally recognized form of consent.

You may generally withdraw or limit consent for future collection, use, or disclosure, subject to legal, professional, contractual, or safety requirements.


Withdrawal of consent does not generally operate retroactively. Information that was lawfully collected, used, or disclosed before withdrawal may continue to be retained where required or permitted by law.


The Nova Scotia PHIA framework recognizes an individual's ability to limit or revoke consent for future uses or disclosures and requires custodians to make reasonable efforts to comply with such requests.

7. When Information May Be Disclosed Without Consent

Confidentiality is fundamental to psychological and counselling services. However, confidentiality is not absolute.


Information may be disclosed without your consent where disclosure is authorized or required by applicable law.


Examples may include circumstances involving:


  • A legal requirement, court order, subpoena, or other lawful authority.
  • Circumstances where disclosure is required to comply with professional or regulatory obligations.
  • Circumstances involving a serious risk of harm where disclosure is legally authorized or required.
  • Suspected abuse or neglect where reporting is legally required.
  • Requirements imposed by child protection legislation.
  • Requirements relating to vulnerable adults where applicable.
  • Regulatory investigations.
  • Law-enforcement requirements where legally authorized.
  • Public-health requirements.
  • emergencies.
  • Legal proceedings involving the practice or professional services.
  • Insurance or benefit administration where legally authorized and appropriately consented to.
  • Other circumstances expressly permitted or required by PHIA or another applicable law.

Only the information reasonably necessary for the permitted or required purpose will be disclosed whenever practicable.

8. Sharing Information With Other Healthcare Providers

With appropriate consent, we may communicate relevant information to other health-care providers involved in your care.


This may include:


  • Family physicians.
  • Psychiatrists.
  • Psychologists.
  • Social workers.
  • Occupational therapists.
  • Nurses.
  • Other regulated health professionals.
  • Other professionals involved in your care.

We use a need-to-know approach and seek to limit disclosures to information reasonably necessary for the purpose.


You may request that we limit or stop certain future disclosures, subject to circumstances where disclosure is required or permitted by law.

9. Family, Couples, and Third-Party Services

Where services involve more than one person, such as couples or family counselling, privacy and confidentiality can involve additional considerations.


At the beginning of such services, the practice will explain applicable confidentiality expectations, including circumstances in which information may be shared between participants or maintained as part of a common clinical record.


Where appropriate, written agreements concerning confidentiality and information sharing will be established.


Individuals should understand that the practice cannot guarantee confidentiality between participants when information is voluntarily shared by one participant with another participant outside the practice.

10. Adolescents

When providing services to children and adolescents, the practice will follow applicable Nova Scotia legislation, professional standards, and the circumstances of the particular client.


The practice will consider:


  • The individual's capacity to provide consent.
  • The role and legal authority of parents, guardians, or substitute decision-makers.
  • The young person's wishes and privacy interests.
  • Applicable legal requirements.
  • The nature of the service.
  • Safety concerns.
  • Any applicable court order or other legal authority.

Information will not automatically be disclosed to a parent or guardian simply because the individual is under the age of majority where applicable law recognizes the young person's ability to make decisions concerning health care.


The specific circumstances of each case will determine the appropriate approach.

11. Protection of Your Information

We implement administrative, physical, and technological safeguards designed to protect personal and personal health information against unauthorized access, collection, use, disclosure, copying, modification, loss, theft, or destruction.


These safeguards may include:


Physical safeguards


  • Locked offices and storage areas.
  • Locked filing cabinets for paper records.
  • Controlled access to clinical areas.
  • Secure disposal containers.
  • Secure destruction of confidential documents.
  • Restrictions on access to areas where client records are stored.
  • Appropriate environmental protections for physical records.

Administrative safeguards


  • Privacy and confidentiality policies.
  • Confidentiality agreements and obligations for staff and contractors.
  • Role-based access to information.
  • Privacy training and education.
  • Procedures for obtaining and documenting consent.
  • Procedures governing disclosure of information.
  • Privacy breach response procedures.
  • Procedures for secure record retention and destruction.
  • Periodic review of privacy and security practices.
  • Disciplinary or corrective procedures for inappropriate access or disclosure.

Technological safeguards


Where appropriate and available, electronic information systems may incorporate:


  • Unique user accounts.
  • Strong passwords.
  • Multi-factor authentication.
  • Role-based access controls.
  • Encryption.
  • Secure network connections.
  • Device security.
  • Firewalls and endpoint protections.
  • Malware protection.
  • System updates and security patches.
  • Secure backups.
  • Audit logs.
  • Access monitoring.
  • Secure data transmission.
  • Secure deletion or destruction procedures.

Nova Scotia's Personal Health Information Regulations specifically require custodians using electronic information systems to implement safeguards addressing network infrastructure, hardware and operating systems, and software and user authentication. Custodians must also maintain written policies supporting those safeguards.

12. Access to Client Records

You generally have the right to request access to your personal health information held by the practice, subject to applicable legal exceptions.


Requests should be made in writing and should provide sufficient information to allow us to identify the records being requested.


Upon receiving a valid request, we will:


  • Verify the identity and authority of the person making the request.
  • Identify the relevant records.
  • Review the request and applicable legal requirements.
  • Provide access where required.
  • Explain any permitted limitation or refusal.
  • Provide information concerning available review or complaint processes where applicable.

Certain information may be subject to lawful exceptions or limitations on access.


For example, professional opinions or observations made in good faith may be subject to specific statutory rules concerning correction. Nova Scotia's PHIA materials specifically recognize that professional opinions made in good faith are not necessarily required to be altered simply because an individual disagrees with them.

13. Correcting Your Information

You may request correction of personal health information that you believe is inaccurate, incomplete, or out of date.


Where a correction is appropriate, the practice will follow the requirements of PHIA and applicable professional standards.


Corrections will not be used to alter or erase a legitimate clinical opinion merely because the individual disagrees with the opinion.


Where appropriate, the record will be amended in a manner that preserves the integrity of the original clinical record and identifies the correction.


Nova Scotia's PHIA framework establishes specific procedures for correcting personal health information and communicating corrections to individuals and, in appropriate circumstances, persons to whom the information was previously disclosed.

14. Electronic Communication

Email, text messaging, telephone, video conferencing, online forms, and other electronic communication methods may involve privacy and security risks.


We take reasonable steps to reduce those risks.


Depending on the circumstances, we may use secure communication systems, encrypted transmission, password-protected systems, secure client portals, or other appropriate safeguards.


However, no electronic communication system can be guaranteed to be completely secure.


You should avoid sending highly sensitive clinical information through ordinary email or text messaging unless the practice has advised you that the particular communication method is appropriate.


When communicating electronically, we may limit the amount of sensitive information included in messages.

15. Telepsychology and Virtual Care

Where psychological or counselling services are provided by telephone, video conference, or another remote technology, we take reasonable steps to protect confidentiality.


Before providing telepsychology services, we may discuss:


  • The technology being used.
  • Privacy and security considerations.
  • The physical location of the client.
  • Emergency contact information.
  • Procedures for dealing with interruptions or technology failures.
  • The limitations of electronic communication.
  • Appropriate environments for confidential sessions.
  • Circumstances in which confidentiality may be affected.

Clients are encouraged to participate from a private location and to use a private, password-protected device and secure internet connection where reasonably possible.


The practice follows applicable professional guidance concerning technology and telepsychology. Current Nova Scotia psychology guidance emphasizes protecting client records, securing electronic information, and taking appropriate care when transmitting information.

17. Payment and Insurance Information

We may collect and use information necessary to process payment and administer insurance or extended health benefits.


Where direct billing is provided, information may be transmitted to the applicable insurer or benefits administrator as necessary to process the claim.

We will disclose only the information reasonably necessary for the applicable billing or insurance purpose.


Payment card information is processed through appropriate payment-service providers where applicable. The practice does not intentionally retain complete payment-card information when it is not necessary for legitimate business purposes.

18. Privacy Breaches

A privacy breach may occur if personal health information is:


  • Lost.
  • Stolen.
  • Accessed by an unauthorized person.
  • Used without authorization.
  • Disclosed without authorization.
  • Copied improperly.
  • Altered improperly.
  • Transmitted to the wrong person.
  • Otherwise handled in a manner that violates applicable privacy requirements.

If a privacy breach occurs, we will take reasonable steps to:


  • Contain the breach.
  • Prevent further unauthorized access or disclosure.
  • Investigate what occurred.
  • Identify the information and individuals affected.
  • Assess the risk associated with the breach.
  • Document the incident.
  • Implement corrective measures.
  • Notify affected individuals where required or appropriate.
  • Notify regulatory or oversight authorities where required.
  • Take reasonable steps to prevent a similar incident from occurring again.

Nova Scotia's PHIA regulations require custodians to maintain records of certain electronic security breaches and document corrective procedures taken to reduce the likelihood of future breaches.


Where a privacy breach creates a legally recognized risk of harm or embarrassment, applicable notification requirements will be followed. Nova Scotia's health privacy framework provides for notification of affected individuals in specified circumstances.

19. Retention of Records

We retain client records for the period required by applicable legislation, professional standards, regulatory requirements, insurance requirements, and other legal obligations.


For psychologists regulated in Nova Scotia, current professional standards provide for retention of individual and corporate client records for at least ten years following the last client contact, subject to specific rules where the client was under 18 at the time of last contact.


Where the client was under 18 at the time of last contact, records are retained for the applicable period following the client's eighteenth birthday, subject to any longer period required by law or professional requirements.


Records may be retained longer where reasonably necessary or required by law, including where:


  • Litigation is anticipated or ongoing.
  • A complaint or regulatory matter is ongoing.
  • An assessment or treatment matter remains active.
  • The record is required for continuity of care.
  • An insurer or other lawful authority requires retention.
  • Another legal or professional requirement applies.
